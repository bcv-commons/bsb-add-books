# Ruth 2: translator's notes

## What this draft is

A machine draft of Ruth 2 in Kuni (kvg), made 14.10.2026 from the Hebrew
(`orig/heb/rut002.txt`, 23 verses, Hebrew numbering = BSB numbering) with the BSB as the only
assisting translation. The drafter knows no Kuni: every word, suffix and construction was taken
from the Kuni NT through `_learn/concord.py`, starting from the store
(`kvg_learning_notes.md`, `profile/observations.json`, `profile/decisions.json`) and from
chapter 1 (`rut001.txt`), whose renderings are reused verbatim wherever the Hebrew repeats
(Moab manqei-ak sævam, Moab manqei-te gunduqavan, tugu zakhapuka, tugu ziwap, Elimelek-qa
bawan-ák, tanakh matev, Evezøza / Mbumbukiam). Confidence per verse: high = every piece is an
attested NT form or a direct substitution in an attested frame; medium = one or two forms built
by analogy (prefix swap for person/gender, optative or prohibitive tone pattern on an attested
stem); low = a construction without a close model. Status: experiment, not reviewed; no footnotes.

## Exegetical observations

- Gleaning (לקט) has no NT word. The NT's "pick the heads of grain" (wit nanga vingisam, Mat 12:1,
  Mrk 2:23, Luk 6:1) is the closest act, so gleaning is "bali nanga vingisam" (picking barley
  heads) throughout; "glean" and "gather" (v7 אסף, v16 לקט) are not distinguished.
- Harvesters (קֹצְרִים) are "sasae ezoqam", the NT's word in Mat 13:30 and Luk 10:2 (workers of the
  harvest); reaping is "pea" (Jhn 4:35, Luk 10:2).
- v1 גִּבּוֹר חַיִל: the BSB's "prominent man of noble character" is rendered with the NT's two
  social labels, "gigiap eve ezoqám" (a man of property, Luk 19:2) and "iz akhae ezoqám" (a man of
  name/standing, Luk 19:12, Act 8:27). "Relative" is "ekeza ezoqam" (36x), the NT's only kin word
  of this kind; "clan" is "bawan" as in chapter 1.
- v2, 10, 13 "find favour in the eyes of": the NT says "kuku ... X-te" (favour/love is toward
  someone: Luk 1:30, 1Co 4:14). v13 uses the blessing form "Kuku até betaqagó, no-te" (may you
  keep having favour toward me), i.e. the wish is phrased from Boaz's side.
- v3 "she happened to come": no word for chance in the NT; "matev āv qanefakhán nqǽgo:" (it
  turned out thus) carries the sense.
- v4 greetings: "The LORD be with you" is 2Ti 4:22's closing blessing; "The LORD bless you" uses
  the NT's "blessed is he" verb (khakheinøv, Jhn 12:13 and parallels, Luk 1:42) with the
  2sg object and the optative be-. The same verb stands in v19 and v20 for בָּרוּךְ.
- v5 "foreman" is described: the servant who looked after the other workers (kewag, 1Ti 5:10).
- v7 "sheaves" (עֳמָרִים): "bali møvøyam" (bundled/stored barley) from the NT's "tie in bundles"
  and "barn" (Mat 13:30); the noun is not attested. Same in v15 and v16 (צְבָתִים).
- v7 "she rested a short time in the shelter": the Hebrew is obscure; the BSB's reading is
  followed with the NT's "rested" (pusa ndøtá, Heb 4:4) and "a short time" (khøuwa elavøqase).
- v8 "Listen": "Nqáe!" is what the NT puts for "Listen!" (Mrk 4:3, 1Co 15:51) as well as "Look!".
- v9 "not to touch you": the order is given as a direct quote, as the NT frames orders
  (Act 16:23), with the attested plural prohibitive "kha mbokhanø̀ze᷄m" (2Co 6:17).
- v9 "go and drink from the jars": "go" is dropped; "you may drink from the jars" uses the NT's
  permission pattern "qo kopømbaqapé, toqo-X" (Luk 5:12).
- v12 "under whose wings you have taken refuge": no word for refuge; made concrete as "you came to
  him and put yourself under his wings" (taf mokho-te, the hen's wings of Luk 13:34; "put into
  my hand" Luk 19:20). "Repay your work" and "full reward" both use yaq-fia (reward, repayment,
  211x), with "Evezøza-te" (from the LORD, Col 3:24).
- v13 "comforted and spoken to the heart": the NT's two idioms, "gave me strength of body"
  (kha føgakh etoam, 2Co 7:6) and "spoke words that push the back" (zita tøpøzu manqat,
  Act 11:23 "encouraged").
- v14 vinegar = "waen khaqain-qape" (sour wine, Jhn 19:29); "morsel" = "lou mbewat" (Jhn 13:26).
  "Roasted grain": "vesu" is attested once ("zonga-kha vesu", broiled fish, Luk 24:42); low.
- v14 "was satisfied": "ewez" with a personal object; in the NT "mbo-ewez" of a person twice means
  "grew weary/aggravated" (Jhn 4:6, Act 16:18) while "zø-ewez" means "were filled" (Luk 9:17,
  Rev 19:21). The form here (vømbō-ewez) follows the "filled" sense; a speaker should check.
- v15 "insult/shame her" = "give her shame" (mivi etoam, 1Co 11:22), with the attested prohibitive
  "mbo-etøòme᷄m" (Eph 4:27). v16 "rebuke" = gugu (Luk 23:40, 9:55), prohibitive built.
- v17 "beat out": the NT's grain-processing phrase "rubbed them, took the husks off" (Luk 6:1)
  stands for threshing by hand; "ephah" = "about one yakhav" (basket, measure: Jhn 6:13, Luk 16:6).
- v20 "not withdrawn his kindness from the living and the dead": turned positive ("still shows
  mercy to the living and the dead"), with chapter 1's "tanakh matev" for חֶסֶד and the NT's
  "khandi ezoqam neka løvøte ezoqam" (2Ti 4:1). "Kinsman-redeemer" (גֹּאֵל) has no term; "one of
  our relatives who are there to redeem us" uses the NT's redeem verb (nqovotaz, Act 7:35,
  1Co 1:30, Luk 1:68). Low; a Kuni legal/kin term may exist.
- v22 "so that nothing will happen to you": near-verbatim Jhn 5:14 ("Soqaín ndøgo, ... tømøqa-fakhan").
- v23 "clung to" (דבק) reuses chapter 1's built "gùmu-tøkenøvém" (v14 there).

## Dialect and style (corpus counts, Kuni NT)

- Divine names as chapter 1: Evezøza for YHWH (vv. 4, 12, 20), Mbumbukiam for Elohim (v12
  "Izlael ezoqam-ge Mbumbukiam", Luk 1:68); in v19 the passive "blessed be" names Mbumbukiam as the
  blesser (the NT formula, Mat 21:9), in v20 Evezøza because the Hebrew says "by the LORD".
- Social words: sasae ezoqam 221 (servant, worker, harvester), sasae mbasønakam 6 / mbasønakam 36
  (girl, servant girl), mbasønakheis 9 (girls), sasae sævam 5 (slave woman), ezoqa ndakinak 22
  (young men), ekeza ezoqam 36 (relative), gigiap eve 6 (rich), iz akhae 25 (of standing),
  manqei vini-ak 16 (foreigner), Khanánqa 19 (Sir), Ngólam 9 (Daughter!), nogu yu 4 (my daughter).
- Farming: wit nanga 43 (heads of grain), vingisam/-øm/-um 10 (pick, gather), bali (barley),
  wit (wheat), avøe gigiap (crops, harvest), khae (field), møvøyam 8 (bundling, storing), yakhav 7
  (basket, measure), ibøkha ziøvem nonqo 3 (water jars), takhaem 3 (draw water), qanaqanus 23
  (morning), khagus 27 (evening), nqægo khaiya 12 (today), atema ndakin 29 (until now).
- Favour, blessing, reward: kuku kandambá 31, bèkhakheinøvém 6 (blessed is he), khakheinøqavem 7
  (you are blessed/healed), yaq-fia 211 (reward), betaqagó 6 (may it be yours), kha føgakh 24
  (comfort), zita tøpøzu 15 (encourage), mivi 80 (shame).
- Speech frames as chapter 1: "āv gumbøe-eín nqǽgo" (she said to him/he said to her, Jhn 18:17,
  19:26), "gumbo-ein", "gendo-qavøiu", "gumbo-qavøiu", "gundu-qavøiu", "gimbo-einim",
  "gembobevap"; orders as a quote after "manqa āv ...-løvuá nqǽgo" (19 verses with manqa + løvua).
- Prohibitives attested as such: ndø-òkho᷄, ndø-ìva᷄v, kha mbokhanø̀ze᷄m, mbo-etøòme᷄m; built:
  mbogùgu᷄. Imperatives attested: Mòqoqáv, Qotøndéqāv, mòqológ, qanøqáv, qanøqóm, vø̀khæ-khofoém;
  built: vø̀qoqóm.
- Headings: no tone marks, verb-final, actors named (as chapter 1).
- Forms built by analogy (not attested as such): gu-vingisam, tu-vingisam, qoqo-vingisam,
  bè-vingisam, ndipeanem, bèkhakheinøqavém, betaqá-etoám, tøqa-papat, qoqonø-omet, qoqonø-ein,
  qoqo-ivøvem, geqa-omet, gundo-itú, gulog, vømbō-ewez, vø̀qoqóm, mbogùgu᷄, toqo-apat,
  gezø-kewagam, bali vingisam khøuwa, bali møvøyam, nqovotaz-qa ndi꞉gú.

## New name forms

None. Boaz = Boáz (glossary, Mat 1:5), as predicate name with the high tone ("Teqa iz Boáz"), bare
"Boaz" elsewhere. Lut, Neomi, Elimelek, Moab, Betliem, Izlael as in chapter 1.

## Open questions

1. Gleaning as "bali nanga vingisam" (picking barley heads) and harvesters as "sasae ezoqam": is
   there a Kuni word for gathering what the reapers leave, and for reapers?
2. "Sheaves" = "bali møvøyam" (vv. 7, 15, 16): the noun is built from "tie in bundles" / "barn"
   (Mat 13:30); a reviewer should supply the real word for a bundle of cut grain.
3. v1 "gigiap eve ezoqám neka iz akhae ezoqám" for גִּבּוֹר חַיִל: wealth and standing, or should
   one of them stand alone?
4. v4 "Evezøza qo bèkhakheinøqavém" (the LORD bless you) and v19/20 "... ndego bèkhakheinøvém"
   (blessed be he): the optative on khakheinøv with a 2sg object is built; check the tones.
5. v12 "refuge under his wings" made concrete as "put yourself under his wings"; v12 "repay"
   and "reward" both with yaq-fia: acceptable?
6. v14 "vømbō-ewez" (was satisfied) may read as "grew weary" (Jhn 4:6); and "vesu" (roasted) rests
   on one verse.
7. v17 "ephah" as "about one yakhav" (basket/measure): is yakhav a usable dry measure?
8. v20 "kinsman-redeemer": "nigi ekeza ezoqa nøme, ndigu ni nqovotaz-qa ndi꞉gú" is a description;
   Kuni kin law may have a term (the one who takes over a dead brother's wife and land).
9. v8/v21 "stay close to" as "namba mba qanøqóm" (stay/sit only with): is qom right for keeping
   company, or should "tøkenøv" (cling, v23) be used in the imperative?
10. The store's reading of gu- as feminine (chapter 1, open question 10): the ꞉-forms gu꞉-/gunu꞉-
    are durative/locative with any subject (Peter "gu꞉qotam" Mat 26:69, Jesus "gunu꞉-apát" Mat 12:1,
    God "gunu꞉gú" 1Jn 4:12); only the plain gu- forms pattern with women. Recorded in the store.
