# Ruth 1: translator's notes

## What this draft is

A machine draft of Ruth 1 in Kuni (kvg), made 14.10.2026 from the Hebrew
(`orig/heb/rut001.txt`, 22 verses, Hebrew numbering = BSB numbering) with the BSB as the only
assisting translation. The drafter knows no Kuni: every word, suffix and construction was taken
from the Kuni NT through `_learn/concord.py`, and the grammar sketch written first is
`export/kvg/kvg_BSB/kvg_learning_notes.md`. Confidence per verse: high = every piece is an
attested NT form or a direct substitution in an attested frame; medium = one or two forms built
by analogy (prefix swap for gender/number, optative or question tone added to an attested
stem); low = a construction I could not find a close model for. Status: experiment, not
reviewed; no footnotes.

## Exegetical observations

- v1 "fields of Moab" (שְׂדֵי מֹואָב, vv. 1, 2, 6, 22): rendered "Moab manqei" (land of Moab) with the
  BSB; the NT glossary already has "Moab manqei" (Balam).
- v1 "to sojourn" (לָגוּר) is spread over two sentences: "went there to live" and, in v2, the
  NT's own phrase for a resident foreigner, "mo-manqei-aním mbá" (Act 7:29).
- v6 "visited his people" (פָקַד): the NT has an exact idiom for this (Luk 1:68, 7:16), taken over.
- v8 חֶסֶד: "tanakh matev" (mercy, compassion) rather than "kuku matev" (love, grace). "tanakh"
  is what the NT uses for the Lord's kindness to a woman (Luk 1:58) and for human compassion
  (Luk 15:20). "matev eqeieqei" (good deeds) would stress the deed; kept uniform as BSB does.
- v12 "tonight" (הַלַּיְלָה): "nqægo lova" (Luk 12:20).
- v13 מַר־לִי מְאֹד מִכֶּם: the BSB's comparative ("more bitter for me than for you") is followed;
  the alternative "bitter for me because of you" would be "zoqa zapaya".
- v13 "the hand of the LORD has gone out against me": the NT renders "hand of the Lord against
  you" (Act 13:11) with "yaq-fia ... ta" (hold judgment / punish), so the idiom, not the
  image, is translated.
- v16 "do not urge me": פגע-ב is pressing/entreating; "pouwe" is the NT's "urge, beg, press"
  (Luk 15:28, 24:29; Act 21:14).
- v16 "wherever you go I will go": the NT formula "Qo ande āv khoqoté-okhoāt, no até
  nøtaqá-pavát" says "I will follow you", which is what the clause means in context.
- v17 the oath formula "thus may the LORD do to me and more" has no NT counterpart; it is
  unpacked into "only death shall part us; if anything else does, may the LORD punish me
  severely". The Hebrew כִּי after the oath is read as "if (anything but) death" with the BSB.
- v17 "I will be buried" is made active-nominal: "my grave will be there" ("manqei ozu",
  Act 2:29), because no passive of "bury" with a 1sg patient is attested.
- v19 "the whole town was stirred": the NT's own rendering of the same idiom (Mat 21:10
  "ezoqa ewaqape nqova zøndáf") is used; "they said" (fem.) is made explicit as "the women of
  the village", as in the BSB.
- v20 "Mara": transliterated "Mala"; the pun (bitter) is not reproducible; no footnotes in this
  experiment.
- v21 "full/empty": "with my family" vs "empty-handed" ("khakha-mbá"), the NT's idiom for being
  sent away with nothing.
- v21 עָנָה בִי: BSB "testified against me"; the Kuni says "spoke about/against me". Other
  readings (afflicted me, humbled me) are possible.
- v22 "Ruth the Moabitess": "Moab manqei-ak sævam" (Moab-land-person woman), after
  "Itiopia manqei-ák" (Act 8:27) and "Samalia sævam" (Jhn 4:7).

- Divine names: Mbumbukiam 1207 alignments for "God", Evezøza 555 for "Lord" (concord lex).
  YHWH = Evezøza, as in the NT's OT quotations (Act 7:49). Shaddai = "Mbumbukiam
  Bazaføgakh-qape" (Jas 5:4 has "Evezøza Bazaføgakh-qape" for "Lord of Hosts"; Rev 1:8 "ndego

## Open questions

1. Ruth's name: the glossary spells the person "Lut" (Deivid entry) but the book "Rut 4:17";
   the project's book title is "Rut". The text uses Lut; the chapter title "Rut" as given.
2. חֶסֶד: "tanakh matev" (mercy) or "kuku matev" (love/grace)? Both are frequent; tanakh was
   chosen for its use with persons (Luk 1:58, 15:20).
3. v13 comparison "more bitter than for you" vs causal "because of you".
4. v16 the prohibitive "nøpòuwe᷄" (don't urge me) and the whole oath in v17 are built, not
   found; a Kuni speaker should check the tone pattern and the oath idiom.
5. v17 "I will be buried": nominal "my grave will be there" acceptable?
6. v20-21 "call me Mara" as "my name shall be Mala" (Luk 1:60 pattern) and "testified against
   me" as "spoke about/against me": both need a reviewer's judgement.
7. "sister-in-law" (v15): "qogu ezoqam" (your female relative) is the closest attested term;
   a more exact kin term may exist outside the NT.
8. "Ephrathites": "Eflata bawan-ak" uses the NT's tribe/clan noun "bawan"; "khagua bawan"
   (Luk 2:36) is the full form for a tribe.
9. v22 "beginning of the barley harvest": "ezoqam bali avøe gigiap pea ndøngazém" (people
   began reaping the barley) is a paraphrase; check whether "avøe" alone means harvest.
10. The gender prefix gu- when a man speaks to a woman (gumbo-ein) and when a woman acts
    (guzø-, gunø-): the sketch assumes gu- marks a feminine participant; worth confirming.
