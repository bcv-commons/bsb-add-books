# Gaps: what the NT has no model for

The NT-only agents recorded 38 gaps while drafting Ruth 1–4. The human Ruth and Jonah now show
how the Kuni translators solved each of them; every gap has a superseding entry (`gap:<id>.h`)
with the human solution, and most point to the term or formula that holds the Kuni form.
Before searching for a word, look here and in the vocabulary: a gap that `answers:` the word
means it has been searched already.

## What the human solutions have in common

1. **Describe, don't coin.** No new nouns for gleaning, threshing floor, sheaf or redeemer:
   "bali khakhayakh pisam" (pick up the left-over barley), "bali nanga tøkuza manqei" (the
   grain-threshing place), "bali løvøtap" (the stalks being tied), "Elimelek-ge ekeza ezoqam"
   (Elimelech's relative). Where our agents built a compound from an NT model (bali evem
   nonqo, bali nqeitam, bali møvøyam), the human chose a different, plainer phrase.
2. **Say the meaning of an image or a custom.** Wings of refuge → "came to him for help";
   "spread your garment over me" → "cover me ... please marry me"; "redeem" → buy the land,
   marry the widow; the hand of the LORD → "the LORD has struck me".
3. **Convert measures.** Ephah and seah are given in kilograms ("Ande āv 10 kilogrém ndægó").
4. **Use the grammar Kuni has.** The passive "I will be buried" is a plain future of bury;
   "do not let him know" is the negative optative (be- + prohibitive tone); a custom is told
   with the habitual -ɨn. Our agents lacked the last two; they are now features.
5. **Merge or split freely.** Two parallel clauses become one (RUT 1:21); a startled awakening
   becomes three short clauses (RUT 3:8).

Our agents' solutions that the human confirmed: the foreman as "the man who looked after the
workers" (kewag), the statement instead of the rhetorical "should I not ...?" (RUT 3:1),
"pusa gétayák" (he will not rest, RUT 3:18), the tone pattern of the built prohibitive (RUT 1:16,
minus the final -t), and the redeemer in narrative as "that relative".

## Gaps that remain for other books

Ruth and Jonah settle these 38. For the next books the likely gaps are cultic and legal terms
(sacrifice types, priest, altar, clean/unclean), kingship and war, and poetry's parallel lines.
Jonah gives a start for sacrifice ("søvakha gigiap ndølaém, Evezøza-te sasa ndøtaem", JON 1:16),
vows ("manqa mbusa", JON 1:16, 2:10), fasting and sackcloth (JON 3:5-8) and the temple ("khoev
mbomambaqape", JON 2:5); check the NT (Hebrews, Luke 1-2) before recording a new gap.
