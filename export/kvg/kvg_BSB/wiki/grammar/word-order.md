# Word order and sentence rhythm

- Verb-final, SOV: "Yesu Betliem vemiav-té geqán" (Mat 2:1), "Ezoqa kopo mø̀ndøgoám"
  (Luk 15:11), "ndego Moab manqei-té genøwáv" pattern from "Yaq Elan taon-té genøwáv" (Act 7:4).
- Many short sentences; one BSB sentence often becomes two or three Kuni ones. Names and
  attributes stand as their own sentences: "Teqa iz Zekalayá." (Luk 1:5), "Tuqa iz, Ená. ...
  Ndugu ngæpuká." (Luk 2:36), "Ngenek Itiopia manqei-ák." (Act 8:27).
- A predicate noun or adjective ends in a high tone: "No lawaqapé." (Luk 1:18), "Ndugu sóg"
  (Luk 1:36), "Manqa-zapazapa mbaín." (Luk 1:6).
- Place phrases are often set off after the verb: "Deivid-qá vemiav-té genøqavíg, Zudia
  plovins-te ndøgo" (Luk 2:4); "vikia seke ndøfakhán, ta manqei-te ndøgo" (Luk 15:14).
- Time setting: "Ta khøuwa-te ndøgo, Elod Zudia plovins-te kawa gegoam, ..." (Luk 1:5, "in the
  days of Herod"); "Khøuwa nøme," (one day, Luk 1:8); "Taqa zita-te," (afterwards, Luk 1:56).

## In Ruth and Jonah (human, 2015)

- **Verb-final** in every clause, with afterthoughts after the verb: "Yaq ngenek ezoqam Moab
  manqei-té genøyág, zas neka nakhei angana menas namba." (RUT 1:1), "Yaq ndigu tinø-qavanát
  ndǿgo, Zuda manqei-te, ziupim-pakha-za namba." (1:7), "Yaq Lut tunøwáv ndǿgo, khae-te, ..." (2:3).
  lexeme-aligner's order profile (cop:order, measured on these books) reads this as Hebrew
  verb-before-object order "kept" 58%; it is the afterthought pattern, not verb-medial order.
- **Topic first, resumed by a pronoun**: "Qogi ezoqam, ndigu nogí ezoqam ndǿgoát." (1:16),
  "Ngenek ezoqam, teqa iz Elimelék." (1:2), "Elimelek, ndego Eflat khagua bawan-ák." (1:2).
- **Sentence length**: 9.0 words per sentence in Ruth, 8.7 in Jonah (NT 9.9); 1.6 Hebrew
  clauses per Kuni sentence. Short sentences of one phrase are normal: "Khøuwa kopó." (one
  day['s walk], JON 3:4), "Mbaín." (RUT 1:11), "Vikia sekeqape ndømát." repeated at the end of
  RUT 1:1.
- **Poetry**: Jonah 2:3-10 is set as `//1` lines, as the NT's Magnificat.
