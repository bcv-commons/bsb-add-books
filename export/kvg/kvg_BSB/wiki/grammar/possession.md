# Possession

The suffix agrees with the possessed noun: -qa (things, general), -ge (a man), -gu (a woman),
-gi (plural).
- "Deivid-gé zeó" (David's son, Luk 2:4), "Saemon-gu zimpuka" (Simon's mother-in-law, Luk 4:38),
  "Klopas-gu zas" (Clopas's wife, Jhn 19:25), "Fanuel-gú yú" (Luk 2:36), "Zekop-gi zi"
  (Luk 1:33), "Pita-qá khoev" (Mat 8:14), "ta vemiav-qa iz" (Luk 2:4).
- Pronoun series: noqa/noge/nogu/nogi (my), qoqa/qoge/qogu/qogi (your sg), teqa/tege/tegu/tegi
  (his), tuqa/tuge/tugu/tugi (her), niqa/nigi (our), zoqa/zogi (your pl), tiqa/tigi (their):
  "tegu zas" (Luk 1:5), "tuqa iz" (Luk 1:27), "tugu namba qawan" (her sister, Jhn 11:5),
  "tugu yu" (Mat 15:28), "nogi nakhei angana neka nakhei mbasi" (2Co 6:18), "qogi bawan ezoqam"
  (Mat 2:6, "your people"), "noge Mbumbukiam neka zoge Mbumbukiam" (Jhn 20:17).
- ekeza (own, reflexive): "ekeza vemiav-té ginøqaváz" (Luk 2:39); nøkeza (my own), qakeza
  (your own, Mrk 8:26), zøkeza (your pl own, 1Co 5:12).
- Inherent kin terms stand bare: "Zivis gèmbonaním" (her husband died, Luk 7:12), "zivis-qa
  avønin-te" (beside her husband, Act 5:10); "qeivis" = your husband (Jhn 4:16).

## In Ruth and Jonah (human, 2015)

- The possessive suffixes are confirmed: "Boaz-qā" (Boaz's [field], RUT 2:3), "Elimelek-ge
  ekeza ezoqam" (-ge, a man, 4:1), "Elimelek-ge yo-pakha-gu zas" (-gu, a woman, 4:5), "Boaz-gi
  sasae ezoqam" (-gi, plural, 2:14), "Pelez-gi zi" (4:12), "Amitai-ge yo" (JON 1:1),
  "zongøyam-qá emu" (the fish's belly, JON 2:1).
- **Kin nouns take -pakha** when possessed (pl -pakha-za), which the NT does not show:
  zakhapuka-pakha, ziwap-pakha, ziupim-pakha-za, sæ-pakha, anganeam-pakha, yo-pakha,
  qakhapikia-pakha (RUT 1:6-2:22). The kin stem can change with the possessor: zakhapuka-pakha
  (her mother-in-law) / qakhapuka-pakha (your, 2:11); zøvøndipakha (your pl parents, 1:8) /
  qavøndipakha (your sg, 2:11). The first version of this page said "inherent kin terms stand
  bare"; that holds for zivis and zas, not for in-laws, children and parents in the OT.
- **ekeza** (own) confirmed: "ekeza ezoqam-té" (to her own people, RUT 1:15), "ekeza mbumbuk-te"
  (to his own god, JON 1:5), "Qakeza Mbumbukiam" (your God, JON 1:6), "nøkeza manqei" (my own
  country, JON 4:2), "Zøkeza zøvøndipakha-té" (to your own parents, RUT 1:8).

> **Question for the Kuni team:** Is -pakha on kin nouns (zakhapuka-pakha, sæ-pakha) still
> current, or was it dropped in the NT revision?

## From the study runs on the NT (2026-10-15, checked by agent:curation-study)

- **The whole reflexive series**: nøkeza (my own), qakeza (your sg own), ekeza (his, her, their
  own), nikeza (our own), zøkeza (your pl own), before the noun, where the possessor is the
  subject: "ekeza paev ezoqam" (his disciples, MRK 1:21), "Qo qakeza ndøwí" (buy it for
  yourself, RUT 4:8).
- **-us = having, full of** makes adjectives from nouns: "bazaføgakh-ús" (powerful, 1CO 1:18),
  "loloakh-us" (wise, 1CO 1:20), "kuku matev-us Mbumbukiám" (JON 4:2). The opposite is
  mbaín (without).
- The **-gu class** is used for a possessed woman, kin or not: evo (mother), zas (wife), yu
  (daughter), sævam, a female servant (GAL 4:22). This is the live `possessor` entry's "-gu (a
  woman)"; the NT adds no exception to check beyond "tugu nqova" (1CO 7:34).
