# Questions

- "Gê, ..." + circumflex on the last word: "Gê, ngenek Zosef-gê yò?" (Luk 4:22), "Neka tegu
  evo, Melî?" (Mat 13:55), "Gê, ezoqa unimanqatin løvøte-te tikhandi꞉z ...?" (Mrk 12:23).
- "Gekha zapâ, ...?" why: "Gekha zapâ, no Evezøza ndozonø-akhayam" (why do you call me Lord,
  Luk 6:46), "Gekha zapâ, no qazonø-vawat?" (Luk 2:49).
- "gekha ezoqâm" who (Mat 21:10); "áv khaténøtēn" how (Luk 1:18).

## In Ruth and Jonah (human, 2015)

- **Who**: "Qo gekha ezoqâm?" (RUT 3:9, without Gê); "whose young woman is this?" = "Ndugu gekha
  mbasønakâm ndunduya?" (2:5); "whose field" = "gekha ezoqam-qâ khae-tē" (2:19).
- **Why**: "Gekha zapâ, ...?" (RUT 1:11, 1:21; JON 1:6, 1:10, 4:10).
- **How did it go**: "Matev áv khaqambé-fakhan?" (RUT 3:16).
- **What shall we do**: "Gê, gekhā nitaqá-gonēm, ...?" (JON 1:11); 'what is your work / land
  / people': "Qo gekhā sasae qógo? Neka qo gekha manqei-âk? Neka gekha bawan-âk?" (JON 1:8).
- **"Ge zô / Ge qô ... matavap"** asks for the hearer's view: "Ge zô ndozo-matavap?" (what do you
  think?, RUT 1:11), "Ge qô ndoqo-matavap." (JON 4:4, 4:9, 4:11) before the real question.
- **Rhetorical questions** whose answer is clear are turned into statements (RUT 2:10, 3:1);
  "Is this Naomi?" stays a question: "Gê, ngunuk unimanqatîn, Naomì?" (1:19).
