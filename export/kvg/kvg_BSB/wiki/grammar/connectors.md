# Connectors, relative clauses, time

- "Yaq" opens most narrative sentences (then, and so): Luk 1:8-12, 2:4-7.
- "neka" joins nouns and clauses (Luk 1:6, 2:16); "nekā" pairs ("Pavpave nekā lovølova").
- "Geté" but (Luk 1:7, 15:28); "gete" inside a clause (1Jn 1:6).
- "Zapa ndǿgo," because / for (Luk 1:7, 2:4); "zapa" + "-qa yaq-te" why/because.
- "Yaq nakémbá." therefore (Luk 1:3, Act 7:34); "Ta zapaya ndøgo" for that reason.
- "av ... ndægo" as (Luk 1:45); "ate av-té" just as (Luk 2:21); "atema" until (Act 7:15
  "ndǿgo teyagám, atema vø̄nanim"; 1Co 4:5 "atema Evezøza vømǿfakhān").
- "nango" / "nqawa" again, back (Luk 1:56, 7:22); "zua" still (Act 7:2, 1Jn 2:9);
  "ndakin" now; "sekemba" any longer / still (Act 21:14 "sekemba pouweáv").
- "ande āv" about, however: "løvøyak ande āv misiká ndi꞉gú" (about three months, Luk 1:56),
  "Qo ande āv khoqoté-okhoāt" (wherever you go, Luk 9:57).
- "ma ... ndøgo" where (relative): "ma Deivid ge꞉yagam" (Jhn 7:42), "Ma tøtegoat ndøgo"
  (where I am, Jhn 7:34), "ma guyagam" (where she lived, Luk 4:26 pattern).

## In Ruth and Jonah (human, 2015)

- **Yaq** is even more frequent than in the NT: 50 per 1000 words in Ruth, 60 in Jonah (NT
  Gospels+Acts 44). Almost every narrative sentence starts with it.
- **"Zapa ndǿgo,"** (because, for) is rare: 1.8 per 1000 in Ruth, 2.3 in Jonah, against 5.2 in
  our drafts. The human uses it for a real reason ("Zapa ndǿgo, Zuda manqei-te ndøgo, lou
  mbaín", RUT 1:1; "Zapa ndǿgo, qo nogé anganeam-pakha-gé ekeza ezoqám", 3:9) and leaves most
  כִּי unmarked. **Geté** (but) is used twice as often as in our drafts.
- **"Yaq nakémbá,"** (therefore, so) in speech (RUT 1:12, 1:13; JON 4:2, 4:3).
- **"até gendego / gundigu / gindigonem,"** brings a participant back to act: "Yaq Ziona até
  gendego, fia vø̄go, ..." (JON 1:3), "Yaq Boaz até gendego, ..." (RUT 4:1, 4:13).
- A chain of vø̄- verbs is often closed by **sasa nd(ø)-**: "Ziona gèndafém, ibøkha-te sasa
  ndø-oginam" (JON 1:15), "Evezøza-te sasa ndøtaem" (JON 1:16), "sasa ndø-eivam" (RUT 1:9).
- **Relative clauses** with a head: head + its pronoun + clause, verb last: "Naomi, ndugu
  sævam, Moab manqei-te gunduqavan" (RUT 4:3), "ndego ezoqam, sasae ezoqa gezø-kewagat" (2:5),
  "Sævam ndugu anganeam tembonanim" (a woman whose husband dies, 2:20), "Mbumbukiam, yan-te
  ngo꞉yage" (JON 1:9). Locative "ma ... ndøgo" is confirmed: "ma te꞉buv ndøgo" (where he lies,
  RUT 3:4), "manqei ndøgo ..., ma gi꞉yagam" (1:7).
- **Comparison**: "av X ndi꞉gu / ngi꞉gu" (like X, RUT 4:11-12), "andé" (as if, 4:17), "av ...
  ndægo" (as, 3:5, JON 3:2), "mø̀ndøløvú" (exceeds, RUT 3:10).
- **Time**: "Qàgoám." opens a book; "Ta khøuwa-te ndøgo," (RUT 1:1, 1:22, 4:7); "Khøuwa nøme,"
  (2:2, 3:1, JON 1:1); "Yaq ta lova ndøgo," (RUT 3:6); "atema" until (RUT 2:17, 3:13, JON 2:7);
  a time clause ends in ndøgo ("lou loge khøuwa qandap ndøgo", when it was time to eat, 2:14).
  Day and night: "Khøuwa misiká neka lova misika." (JON 2:1); "nqægo khøuwa" (today, RUT 3:18,
  4:9, 4:10; our 'nqægo khaiya' is not used); midnight "lova u mbá" (3:8).
