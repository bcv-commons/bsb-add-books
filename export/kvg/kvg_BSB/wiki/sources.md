# Sources and how far to trust them

What each source of Kuni knowledge is, and what it is good for. The Kuni texts are not ours: the eBible
text is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (© 2015 Wycliffe Switzerland), the NT here a newer working copy. This wiki quotes
them in short passages from the published text (`../CREDITS.md`, docs/licensing.md); the rest is CC0.

## The Kuni texts

| text | where | state | trust |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kuni NT | `import/non-free/kvg/KVG-KuNT/text/` (27 books) | a **newer revision** than eBible's, received from the team | the reference for spelling, tone marks and NT terms |
| Ruth, Jonah | same folder, `rut001`–`rut004`, `jon001`–`jon004` | **eBible 2015**, Hebrew verse numbering (JON 2:1 = BSB 1:17) | the only human OT: the reference for OT style, names and OT vocabulary |
| glossary | `glossary.tsv` (`concord.py gloss`) | with the NT | explains key terms in Kuni; words marked `*` in the text |

The two layers differ a little, because the NT was revised after 2015:

- spelling: Jonah writes "qanaqanus lovalova-us" (JON 4:7), the NT "lovølova"; Ruth writes
  "gemanaz", the revised Mat 1 "gendomanaz"; Ruth's "-pakha" kin forms (zakhapuka-pakha,
  sæ-pakha) do not occur in the NT outside evøndipakha (parents);
- the divine name: Ruth writes *Yawe (18×, with the glossary star but no glossary entry),
  Jonah Evezøza, the NT Evezøza for the Lord (see [names](names.md));
- tone marking may have been revised; where the NT and Ruth/Jonah disagree on a form, prefer
  the NT's spelling and Ruth/Jonah's choice of words, and note the case.

## The lexicons (`concord.py lex` and `hlex`)

- `lex <english>`: eflomal alignment of the BSB with the Kuni NT + Ruth + Jonah (8,065 verse
  pairs). The better of the two aligners: its top rendering matches the glossary for 75% of
  one-word terms (IBM Model 2: 64%); names of Ruth/Jonah right in 24 of 28. Function words
  and multi-word renderings are its weak spot: an English word often maps to a fragment of a
  Kuni phrase ("bless" → ndøgoat). Use it to find candidates, then read the verses.
- `hlex <hebrew>`: Hebrew lexeme → Kuni, from Ruth + Jonah only (133 verse pairs). Rough; good
  for names and for checking how the human rendered a lexeme, poor for anything rare.
- The NT-only lexicons used for the held-out test are kept in `_learn/nt-only/`.

## lexeme-aligner's data (`download/lexeme-aligner/grammar/`)

- **gram-struct** (`gs_kvg.json`): WALS codes (postpositions 85A, OV 83A, SV 82A) agree with
  the corpus; the alignment-derived slots say "mixed" for adposition, object-verb and
  possessor, which is an artefact (suffixed postpositions, object prefixes, afterthought
  phrases, only 58 possessor cases). Trust the WALS values and the corpus over `derived`.
- **constituent-order profile** (`cop_kvg_wbt.json`): measured on Ruth + Jonah (130 verses).
  Its high "kept" rates for Hebrew verb-before-object (58%) reflect Kuni's short clauses and
  post-verbal phrases, not verb-medial order.
- **target-morphology** (`tm_kvg.json`): affix inventories agree with our verb prefixes and
  negation suffixes and show -ɨn as a frequent suffix; tone variants count as separate
  affixes, so it is a check, not a parser.
- **stopwords** (`ts_kvg.txt`): the function words (yaq, geté, ndøgo, neka, av, nqægo ...);
  useful to ignore them in searches.
- **Known errors** (from `_eval/alignment_report.md`): some of lexeme-aligner's Ruth rows are
  off: `lut` → "he said", `zo` → "person", `qo` → "covenant loyalty"; Ruth → *moab*, Perez →
  *atanakha*; Amittai → *yo* (from "Amitai-ge yo", also in our aligners), Tamar → *zi*; Joppa
  (Ziopa) is missed by all three aligners. Their NT alignment also used Ruth/Jonah statistics.
  Of their eight misses on the glossary check only copper and aloes are real (rare words); the
  rest are function words in MACULA (amen, hallelujah) or one Greek lexeme for two Kuni words.
  (Our own name gold for that check had two wrong forms, Yahweh and Rachel.)

## Our own earlier knowledge

The store entries by `agent:rut001`–`agent:rut004` and the first version of these pages were
learned from the NT alone, while Ruth and Jonah were held out. Where the human Ruth/Jonah
differ, an entry by `agent:curate-rut-jon` supersedes them (the store keeps both; the merged
view shows the latest). The NT-only learners were right on the grammar (prefixes, tone of the
prohibitive, conditionals) and on half the vocabulary; they were wrong mostly where the OT
needs words the NT lacks (harvest, threshing, redemption, measures) and on name spellings.
