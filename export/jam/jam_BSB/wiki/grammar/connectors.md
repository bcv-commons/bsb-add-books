# Speech, connectors, prepositions, time

## Speech: se … se

The frame has **se** twice: the verb *se* (say) and the complementizer *se* before the quotation:
"Jiizas se tu dem se, “…”" (LUK 15:7; *se tu X se* 355×). Also "Jiizas ansa im se" (MAT 4:7),
"aks dem … se", "tel im se". *se,* stands before an opening quote mark 1,472×. Hebrew וַיֹּאמֶר … לֵאמֹר
fits this exactly: the first verb is *se*, לֵאמֹר is the second *se*.

Quotation marks: “ ” for speech, ‘ ’ inside, “ ” again at the third level (MAT 22:44).

Sentence-initial words: An 1,037, So 934, Bot 893, Wen 618, Den 436, Nou 258. In Hebrew narrative
(inferred from these figures, not from an OT text): do not open every clause with *An*; join two or
three actions with *an* and start a new sentence with *So*, *Den* or *Wen*.

## Prepositions

**iina** (in, into; 3,060×), **a** (at, to a place: "go a yu yaad", LUK 15:6), **pan** (on), wid,
fram, tu (to a person), outa, bout, uova, anda, **infronta** (before, in the presence of), mongks
(among), gens (against), saida (beside). The priors' *ina*, *ova* are absent: write *iina*, *uova*.

## Time

"Wen taim did staat" (In the beginning, JHN 1:1); "fram Gad mek di worl" (from the creation, MAT
19:4); "wen it kech iivnin" (MRK 4:35); *wan die*, *di neks die*, *afta dat*. "Die an nait" (REV 4:8).

## Attention words

Behold: "Luk ya" (22×), "wach ya nou" (MAT 1:20), "Lisn op!" (MAT 1:23), "Wach it" (HEB 8:8).
Hebrew הִנֵּה is much more frequent than these: leave it out in narrative, use one of them in speech.
"Matarafak" = in fact (JHN 1:1).
