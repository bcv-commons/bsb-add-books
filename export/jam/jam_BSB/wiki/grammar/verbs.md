# Verbs: tense, aspect, mood

Jamaican verbs never change their form. Everything English does with endings (-ed, -s, -ing, was, will)
is done here with small words **before** the verb, or with nothing at all. The commonest error of a
drafter who knows English is to reach for English tense: there is no *-ed*, no *was*, no *will*.

## The past in a story: the bare verb

The main line of a narrative uses the bare verb: "Jiizas ben dong an staat rait" (JHN 8:6), "Shi rap im
op" (LUK 2:7), "lef im konchri" (ACT 7:4). Hebrew wayyiqtol chains become bare verbs joined by *an*
or started anew with *So*, *Den*, *Wen* (see [connectors](connectors.md)).

The derived report proposed verb endings (-v present, -k imperative, -t passive …) from word-final
letters. They are an artefact of an isolating language and are **rejected**: there are no verb endings.

## did: past of states and backgrounds

*did* (3,513×) is not the narrative past. It marks:
- a past **state**: did de (was there, 225×), did av (had, 170×), did waahn (wanted), did wel uol
  (LUK 1:7);
- a **background or earlier** event, a pluperfect: "Iebriyam did bai da piis a lan de" (ACT 7:16, the
  purchase long before the burial);
- the **past copula** with *a*: "Iebriyam did a Aizak faada" (MAT 1:2), "Aizak a did Iebriyam
  pikni" (LUK 3:34).

*did a* + verb is the past progressive: "mi did a chrimbl" (1CO 2:3), "Piipl did a nyam, jringk"
(LUK 17:27). Read *did a* by what follows: a verb = progressive, a noun = copula.

## Present, progressive, future, completive

- **a** + verb: ongoing or habitual: "Mi a tel unu" (LUK 15:7), "Mi a fala Paal" (1CO 1:12).
  A plain state is the bare verb: *mi nuo*, *im lov*.
- **ago** (1,573×), **gweehn** (119×), **wi** + verb: future. "Mi ago shuo" (1CO 1:19), "Gad gweehn
  siev" (1TI 2:15), "Mi wi bi unu faada" (2CO 6:18). Negative: **naa go** (307×).
- **don** + verb: already done (1CO 1:7 "don gi unu"). Rare; most English perfects are bare verbs.

## Modals

kyan (can) / kyaahn (can't), kuda / kudn, wuda / wudn, shuda, mos, **afi** (have to, 286×), maita
(might). After a subject, **fi** = should: the Law's commandments in the NT are "Yu fi lov di Laad"
(MRK 12:30), "Yu fi worship" (MAT 4:10). Use *Yu fi* for Hebrew commands in the Law; plain
imperatives for direct orders.

## Let us, let there be: mek

- "let us" = **Mek wi** + verb: 32 of the 52 BSB "let us" verses (MRK 1:38 "Mek wi go"; 1JN 4:7);
  10 more start with *Kom* (Kom mek …, JHN 11:16). The derived report's "nothing" for the 1st plural
  subjunctive was wrong.
- God's jussives in Genesis 1 ("Let there be light") have their model in 2CO 4:6, God's own word
  quoted: "Mek di lait shain". Build *Mek* + subject + verb: *Mek lait de* (by rule).
- *mek* is also the causative: "mek wi kyan nuo Gad" (enabled us to know God, 1CO 1:30).

## Prohibitive

"No" + verb: "No fried!" (LUK 1:13, 38×), "No tes di Laad" (MAT 4:7); "no bada" + verb (don't
bother to, ROM 12:19).

## Serial verbs

Verbs follow each other without *and* or *to*: "go a Betliyem go si" (LUK 2:15), "kom kil it"
(LUK 15:23), "kom kom tel" (came to tell, 1PE 1:12). *gi* after an object means "to/for": "bon …
sakrifais gi Gad" (HEB 11:4).

## Passive

There is none. Use (1) **get** + verb: "get siev" (1CO 10:33), "get baptaiz" (1CO 1:15); (2) the
patient as subject of the bare verb: "Adam did mek bifuo Iiv" (Adam was formed first, 1TI 2:13); or
(3) impersonal *dem*: "di giet we dem kaal…" (ACT 3:2). Hebrew Niphal "was called" = *dem kaal* or
*we niem*.
