Genesis 33 (gen033), Kuni: notes on the draft

What this is. A machine draft of Genesis 33 (20 verses, Hebrew numbering = BSB numbering) in Kuni, translated from the Hebrew (orig/heb/gen033.txt) with the BSB as the only assisting translation. The agent does not know Kuni: every word and construction was looked up in the Kuni team's Genesis 1–30, Ruth, Jonah and the NT, the verse pack and the wiki. Status: experiment, not reviewed. Source of the text: gen033.tagged (the .txt and .align are generated from it).

Style model. Our Genesis 32 draft (read in full, with its notes) for the thread, names and decided terms (gift = etoam matev, favour = tanakh, group = bawan, Khanánqa); our Genesis 31 draft; the team's Genesis 18–19 and 23 (bowing, Lot urging his guests, buying the field), 24:65 and 29:13 (a meeting, embracing and kissing), 26:25 and 12:7 (tent and altar), 21:31 and 28:19 (naming a place with its meaning in brackets). Short sentences opened by Yaq or Geté, verb last; long frames (āv … nqǽgo) for Esau's two questions and Jacob's formal plea (v13), short frames elsewhere.

Not found in the corpus (built, or rendered by meaning)

  • seven times (v3): numeral before the verb, "seven gèkhafutáv" (as LUK 17:4); gen031 built gèmbo-ten. Both are open.
  • driven hard (v13): "if I lead them fast" (nakhamas-té tæzø-itub); in my care (v13): "with me"; at the pace of (v14): "as they walk" (tiqa okho ndægo).
  • my brother as a vocative (v9): Áta! built from ata-za (ACT 7:2) by the vocative rule.
  • fell on his neck (v4): not rendered separately; the embrace sège-møvøiú (29:13) covers it.
  • Built verb forms (patterns attested, these forms not seen): genøgo (he was kind to me), qoqotøndo-khofotáz (you sent them here), toqonøgo (as gen032), gèmbo-pouweám (he urged him), tæzø-itub (if I lead them), bezøgó for "let me leave" (the form is attested, the use is built), ndøpuan in a relative clause.

New name forms

  • Sukot (Succoth, v17): k for kk, th → t; with manqei; meaning "(Taqa manqa mokho: Khoev-qasis.)" (little houses, the NT's shelters).
  • El-Eloe-Izlael (El-Elohe-Israel, v20): inner h dropped (as Bila, Zilpa); meaning "(Taqa manqa mokho: Mbumbukiam, ndego Izlael-ge Mbumbukiam.)".
  • Hamo (Hamor, v19) is the NT's form (ACT 7:16), not new.

Fixed renderings kept from gen032

Gift = etoam matev; favour = tanakh (toqonøgo / qoqonøgo); company = bawan; my lord = Khanánqa; your servant = "no, qoge sasae ezoqam"; 400 men = ezoqam fo andléd; returned = genøqaván.

Open questions for the Kuni team

  1. YHWH: not in this chapter. God = Mbumbukiam (v5, 10, 11, 20).
  2. "My lord" to Esau: the draft uses the vocative Khanánqa and "you" (as Ruth 2:13), not a third person. Right for a brother speaking with deference?
  3. "My brother!" (v9): is Áta! the vocative?
  4. Seven times (v3): how does Kuni say "N times"?
  5. Drive hard, in my care, pace (v13–14): what are the words?
  6. Shalem (v18): the draft follows the BSB "safely" (sambi-té); the Hebrew can also be read as a town name.
  7. Succoth and El-Elohe-Israel: are the spellings and the bracketed meanings right?
  8. Built forms above, especially genøgo and gèmbo-pouweám.

Least sure

v13 and v14 (driving the flocks, pace), v15 (let me leave people with you), v9 (vocative), v3 (seven times).
