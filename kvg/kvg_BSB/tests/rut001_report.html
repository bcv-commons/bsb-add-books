Kuni Ruth 1: the first drafting test (2026-10-14)

Evaluator's report. This folder (_eval/) is on the agents' do-not-open list because it holds scores against the held-out Kuni Ruth; the agents must not learn from it.

Setup

  • Language: Kuni (kvg), Kuni-Boazi, Papua New Guinea. No LLM exposure.
  • What the agent had: the Kuni NT (import/non-free/kvg/KVG-KuNT/text/), its 156-term glossary, the baseline alignment lexicons (_learn/lexicon_eng.tsv, lexicon_grc.tsv, built from the NT only) and the learning tool _learn/concord.py. The Hebrew (orig/heb/rut001.txt, clause lines) and the BSB as the only assisting translation.
  • What it must not see: the Kuni Ruth and Jonah (holdout/), lexeme-aligner's data, scratch/, download/, this folder.
  • Run: one fresh agent, prompt from make_prompt.py kvg/kvg_BSB rut 1; 25 minutes, 39 tool calls. It first wrote a grammar sketch from the NT (kvg_learning_notes.md, 277 lines; superseded by wiki/, kept locally in _eval/local/), then the draft and its notes.
  • Score: chrF (character n-grams 1–6, β=2) against the published Kuni Ruth 1 (an early version of scripts/score.py), tone marks ignored unless stated.

All 22 verses present; check_draft.py reports no problems. The draft beats the strongest no-learning baseline by six points: the agent learned Kuni structure from the NT, not just words.

Speech verses with little NT precedent are the weak part: conditionals ("if I should have a husband tonight"), the oath of 1:17, "the LORD has testified against me". The agent's own "least sure" list (1:13, 1:16a, 1:17, 1:21) overlaps with the low scorers, so its confidence is informative.

What the agent produced beyond the draft

  • Grammar sketch (kvg_learning_notes.md): SOV, short sentences chained with "Yaq"; subject prefixes ge-/gu-/gi- (gu- for a feminine participant); directionals; sequential vø̄-; perfective mø̀ndø-; negation -áv/-ák; prohibitive tone patterns; optative be-/bezø-; possessive suffixes agreeing with the possessed noun; the speech frame "āv gene-eín nqǽgo"; name rules (r→l, j→z, th→t, h dropped). Each point with NT references.
  • Names: Neomi, Elimelek, Malon, Kilion, Opa (Orpah), Mala; from the glossary/NT: Lut (Ruth), Moab, Betliem, Zuda. YHWH = Evezøza, Elohim = Mbumbukiam, Shaddai = Mbumbukiam Bazaføgakh-qape.
  • Open questions for a Kuni speaker: chesed (tanakh vs kuku); "call me Mara" rendered as "my name shall be Mala" (Luke 1:60 pattern); "sister-in-law" as "qogu ezoqam"; Lut (glossary) vs Rut (book title); 1:13 comparative vs causal.

Comparison with lexeme-aligner

Their kvg_wbt alignment is right on 69 of 77 one-word glossary terms (our baseline: 64); corrected for errors of the English-to-Strong's bridge, about 73. Their NT alignment used some statistics from the Kuni Ruth/Jonah, so the agent was given only our NT-only lexicon. Their constituent-order-profile for Kuni is computed from Ruth and Jonah (130 verses) and is therefore off limits for this test.
