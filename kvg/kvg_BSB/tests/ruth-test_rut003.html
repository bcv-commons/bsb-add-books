1 Khøuwa nøme, Lut-gu zakhapuka Neomi, Lut āv gumbøe-eín nqǽgo, “Ngólam. No qo-te pusup manqei vawa-qa nombogó. Yaq qo eqeieqei qóyagát.
וַתֹּ֥אמֶר לָ֖הּ נָעֳמִ֣י חֲמוֹתָ֑הּ בִּתִּ֞י הֲלֹ֧א אֲבַקֶּשׁ־לָ֛ךְ מָנ֖וֹחַ אֲשֶׁ֥ר יִֽיטַב־לָֽךְ׃
BSB 3:1 One day Ruth’s mother-in-law Naomi said to her, “My daughter, should I not seek a resting place for you, that it may be well with you?
וַתֹּאמֶר לָהּ נָעֳמִי חֲמוֹתָהּ → Khøuwa nøme, Lut-gu zakhapuka Neomi, Lut āv gumbøe-eín nqǽgo (One day Ruth's mother-in-law Naomi said to her)
"Khøuwa nøme," (one day, Luk 1:8); "Saemon-gu zimpuka" (Simon's mother-in-law, Luk 4:38) → Lut-gu zakhapuka; frame "āv gumbøe-eín nqǽgo" (ch2 v2). High.
בִּתִּי הֲלֹא אֲבַקֶּשׁ־לָךְ מָנוֹחַ → Ngólam. No qo-te pusup manqei vawa-qa nombogó (My daughter, should I not seek a resting place for you)
"Ngólam." (Mrk 5:34); resting place = "pusup manqei" (God's rest, Heb 4:3, 4:1); seek = vawa ("Sol-qa ndøvawát" Act 11:25, "ndøvawét" Act 10:19); "I want to X" = "X-qa nombogó" (Act 25:22 "yoge-qa nombogó", 1Co 15:1 "eim-qa nøzøgó"). The rhetorical question is a statement of intent (Luk 13:16 turns a "should not" question into statements). Low (vawa-qa nombogó built; the question lost).
אֲשֶׁר יִיטַב־לָךְ → Yaq qo eqeieqei qóyagát (that it may be well with you)
Verbatim Eph 6:3 "that it may go well with you". High.
2 Ndakin, nqáe! Boaz, ndego tegi sasae mbasønakheis namba, qo sasae qoqogoam, ndego nigi ekeza ezoqám. Nqægo lova, ndego bali evem nonqo-te, bali ndǿ-evém.
וְעַתָּ֗ה הֲלֹ֥א בֹ֨עַז֙ מֹֽדַעְתָּ֔נוּ אֲשֶׁ֥ר הָיִ֖ית אֶת־נַעֲרוֹתָ֑יו הִנֵּה־ה֗וּא זֹרֶ֛ה אֶת־גֹּ֥רֶן הַשְּׂעֹרִ֖ים הַלָּֽיְלָה׃
BSB 3:2 Now is not Boaz, with whose servant girls you have been working, a relative of ours? In fact, tonight he is winnowing barley on the threshing floor.
הֲלֹא בֹעַז מֹדַעְתָּנוּ אֲשֶׁר … → Ndakin, nqáe! Boaz, ndego tegi sasae mbasønakheis namba, qo sasae qoqogoam, ndego nigi ekeza ezoqám (Now is not Boaz, with whose servant girls you have been working, a relative of ours?)
"Ndakin" (Act 9:6), "Nqáe!" (Luk 1:20); the relative as an appositive clause after the name, "X, ndego <clause>, tege ekeza ezoqam" (Jhn 18:26); "sasae qoqogoam" (you worked, ch2 v19); "nigi ekeza ezoqám" (ch2 v20). The "is not?" becomes "Look!" + statement. Medium.
הִנֵּה־הוּא זֹרֶה אֶת־גֹּרֶן הַשְּׂעֹרִים הַלָּיְלָה → Nqægo lova, ndego bali evem nonqo-te, bali ndǿ-evém (tonight he is winnowing barley on the threshing floor)
"nqægo lova" tonight (Luk 12:20); threshing floor = "wit evem nonqo" (Luk 3:17, Mat 3:12) → "bali evem nonqo" by analogy; "wit nanga ndǿ-evém" (he will winnow the wheat, Luk 3:17) → "bali ndǿ-evém" (the participle rendered as a future). Medium (bali evem nonqo built).
3 Yaq nakémbá, qakeza kha vø̀sungúz, neka tae et sisip mbomambaqape qakeza kha-te vø̄qouz, neka qoqa ndabua eqeieqei vø̄-uz. Yaq bali evem nonqo-té qonǿvís. Geté ta ezoqam ndøgo, qo mè꞉-otevák, atema lou loge neka izam vømǿ-navøēm.
וְרָחַ֣צְתְּ׀וָסַ֗כְתְּ וְשַׂ֧מְתְּ שִׂמְלֹתַ֛יִךְ עָלַ֖יִךְ וְיָרַ֣דְתְּ הַגֹּ֑רֶן אַל־תִּוָּדְעִ֣י לָאִ֔ישׁ עַ֥ד כַּלֹּת֖וֹ לֶאֱכֹ֥ל וְלִשְׁתּֽוֹת׃
BSB 3:3 Therefore wash yourself, put on perfume, and wear your best clothes. Go down to the threshing floor, but do not let the man know you are there until he has finished eating and drinking.
וְרָחַצְתְּ וָסַכְתְּ וְשַׂמְתְּ שִׂמְלֹתַיִךְ עָלַיִךְ → Yaq nakémbá, qakeza kha vø̀sungúz, neka tae et sisip mbomambaqape qakeza kha-te vø̄qouz, neka qoqa ndabua eqeieqei vø̄-uz (Therefore wash yourself, put on perfume, and wear your best clothes)
"Yaq nakémbá, mòqoqáv" (Act 7:34); wash = sunguz ("zøsungúz" Act 16:33, "gèsunguzím" Act 9:37), "qakeza kha" yourself (Act 16:28); perfume = "tae et sisip mbomambaqape" (Jhn 12:3, Luk 7:46), anoint = qouz ("mo꞉qóuz" Luk 7:46, "ande oil me gembøe-qouz" Act 10:38); "ndabua eqeieqei" fine clothes (Luk 7:25), "Qoqa ndabua qozaq nonqo vø̄-uz" (put on your cloak, Act 12:8). Imperative chain vø̀-/vø̄- as Act 22:16, Act 9:34. Medium (vø̀sungúz, vø̄qouz built).
וְיָרַדְתְּ הַגֹּרֶן → Yaq bali evem nonqo-té qonǿvís (Go down to the threshing floor)
"Manqei-té qonǿvís" (go downstairs, Act 10:20). High.
אַל־תִּוָּדְעִי לָאִישׁ עַד כַּלֹּתוֹ … → Geté ta ezoqam ndøgo, qo mè꞉-otevák, atema lou loge neka izam vømǿ-navøēm (do not let the man know you are there until he has finished eating and drinking)
"Ezoqa mè꞉-otevák" (no one knows, 1Co 2:11: negative future of otev, notice/understand) → "the man must not notice you"; "lou loge matev neka izam matev" (eating and drinking, 1Co 10:7); "atema ... vømǿ-navøēm" (until they finish, ch2 v21). Low (the jussive passive has no model).
4 Yaq ndego sømbiam-qa tewav, ta manqei ndøgo, ma ndego tesømbiam, eqeieqei gè-ometé. Yaq ndǿgo qanøqáv, teqa zenda tokhotapak-te ndabua vø̀khofoé, neka ndǿgo vø̄sømbiam. Yaq ndego ndø̀qa-eín, qo ande āv khoqotégō.”
וִיהִ֣י בְשָׁכְב֗וֹ וְיָדַ֨עַתְּ֙ אֶת־הַמָּקוֹם֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר יִשְׁכַּב־שָׁ֔ם וּבָ֛את וְגִלִּ֥ית מַרְגְּלֹתָ֖יו וְשָׁכָ֑בְתְּ וְהוּא֙ יַגִּ֣יד לָ֔ךְ אֵ֖ת אֲשֶׁ֥ר תַּעַשִֽׂין׃
BSB 3:4 When he lies down, note the place where he lies. Then go in and uncover his feet, and lie down, and he will explain to you what you should do.”
וִיהִי בְשָׁכְבוֹ וְיָדַעַתְּ אֶת־הַמָּקוֹם … → Yaq ndego sømbiam-qa tewav, ta manqei ndøgo, ma ndego tesømbiam, eqeieqei gè-ometé (When he lies down, note the place where he lies)
lie down = sømbi (sleep, lie: "sømbiam nonqo" Luk 9:58, "sasa ndøsømbiam" Mat 25:5); "X-qa tewav" (goes to X), "ta manqei ndøgo, ma ..." (ch1 v7); "gè-ometé" (take note of him, 2Th 3:14) with the place as object; "eqeieqei" carefully (2Pe 1:19). Low (tesømbiam, the object of gè-ometé built).
וּבָאת וְגִלִּית מַרְגְּלֹתָיו וְשָׁכָבְתְּ → Yaq ndǿgo qanøqáv, teqa zenda tokhotapak-te ndabua vø̀khofoé, neka ndǿgo vø̄sømbiam (Then go in and uncover his feet, and lie down)
"qanøqáv" go (Jhn 20:17); uncover = take the cloth off his feet: "Qoqa tamak vø̀khofoé" (take off your sandals, Act 7:33), "zenda tokhotapak" feet (Luk 7:44); chained imperative "neka ... vø̄løvøen" (Act 9:34) → vø̄sømbiam. Medium (vø̄sømbiam built).
וְהוּא יַגִּיד לָךְ אֵת … → Yaq ndego ndø̀qa-eín, qo ande āv khoqotégō (he will explain to you what you should do)
Verbatim Act 9:6 ("you will be told what you must do"). High.
5 Yaq Lut gundu-qavøiu, “Manqat ate ndægo, qo toqonø-ein, no ndæ̀matønumát.”
וַתֹּ֖אמֶר אֵלֶ֑יהָ כֹּ֛ל אֲשֶׁר־תֹּאמְרִ֥י אֵלַ֖י אֶֽעֱשֶֽׂה׃
BSB 3:5 “I will do everything you say,” Ruth answered.
כֹּל אֲשֶׁר־תֹּאמְרִי אֶעֱשֶׂה → Manqat ate ndægo, qo toqonø-ein, no ndæ̀matønumát (I will do everything you say)
"Gekha matēv ndøgo, zo ... tozo-viømemat, no ndæ̀matønumát" (whatever you ask I will do, Jhn 14:13); "Qo toqonø-ein" (you tell me, Mrk 6:23); "manqat ate qægoam" all the words (Act 10:8); "gundu-qavøiu" she replied (Jhn 4:17). High.
6 Yaq ndugu bali evem nonqo-té gunøvís. Yaq ndugu matev ate ndægo ndømatanám, av tugu zakhapuka manqa gumbøe-løvua.
וַתֵּ֖רֶד הַגֹּ֑רֶן וַתַּ֕עַשׂ כְּכֹ֥ל אֲשֶׁר־צִוַּ֖תָּה חֲמוֹתָֽהּ׃
BSB 3:6 So she went down to the threshing floor and did everything her mother-in-law had instructed her to do.
וַתֵּרֶד הַגֹּרֶן וַתַּעַשׂ כְּכֹל … → bali evem nonqo-té gunøvís. Yaq ndugu matev ate ndægo ndømatanám, av tugu zakhapuka manqa gumbøe-løvua (she went down to the threshing floor and did everything her mother-in-law had instructed)
"tenøvís" (he went down, Act 10:21), "ginøvis" (they went down) → gunøvís; "ndømatanám" did (Mat 1:20); "av ... geqambe-matanam" (as he did for you, Mrk 5:19); "manqa gezø-løvua" ordered (Heb 11:28) → gumbøe-løvua (she ordered her). Medium (gunøvís, gumbøe-løvua built).
7 Yaq Boaz lou loge neka izam qamømbøe-navøem, yaq ndego khanakhanakh ndøndáp. Yaq ndego bali nqeitam-qa vaev-té genøwáv, ndǿgo genø-sømbiám. Yaq Lut khonoam-te ndowáv, teqa zenda tokhotapak-te ndabua vø̄khofoe, ndǿgo sasa ndøsømbiam.
וַיֹּ֨אכַל בֹּ֤עַז וַיֵּשְׁתְּ֙ וַיִּיטַ֣ב לִבּ֔וֹ וַיָּבֹ֕א לִשְׁכַּ֖ב בִּקְצֵ֣ה הָעֲרֵמָ֑ה וַתָּבֹ֣א בַלָּ֔ט וַתְּגַ֥ל מַרְגְּלֹתָ֖יו וַתִּשְׁכָּֽב׃
BSB 3:7 After Boaz had finished eating and drinking and was in good spirits, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain. Then Ruth went in secretly, uncovered his feet, and lay down.
וַיֹּאכַל בֹּעַז וַיֵּשְׁתְּ וַיִּיטַב לִבּוֹ → Boaz lou loge neka izam qamømbøe-navøem, yaq ndego khanakhanakh ndøndáp (After Boaz had finished eating and drinking and was in good spirits)
"Yesu manqa manqate qamømbøe-navøem, yaq ..." (when Jesus had finished speaking, Luk 5:4); "lou loge ... neka izam" (1Co 10:7); "khanakhanakh ndøndáp" (she was overjoyed, Act 12:14; "my heart is glad" Act 2:26). High.
וַיָּבֹא לִשְׁכַּב בִּקְצֵה הָעֲרֵמָה → ndego bali nqeitam-qa vaev-té genøwáv, ndǿgo genø-sømbiám (he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain)
heap: no NT word; "bali nqeitam" (stored/piled barley) from nqeit (store: "lou nqeitam nonqo khoev" barn, Luk 3:17; "ginqeitømem" filled baskets, Jhn 6:13); "vaev-té genø-ámb" (placed at the end, 1Co 4:9); "ginø-sømbiám" (they lay sleeping, Mat 26:43) → genø-sømbiám. Low (bali nqeitam built).
וַתָּבֹא בַלָּט וַתְּגַל מַרְגְּלֹתָיו וַתִּשְׁכָּב → Lut khonoam-te ndowáv, teqa zenda tokhotapak-te ndabua vø̄khofoe, ndǿgo sasa ndøsømbiam (Ruth went in secretly, uncovered his feet, and lay down)
"khonoam-te" in secret (Act 16:37, Eph 5:12); "ndowáv" came (Luk 7:16); "ekeza ndabua vø̄khofoe" (threw off their cloaks, Act 22:23); "venanqei sasa ndøsømbiam" (fell asleep, Mat 25:5). High.
8 Yaq qamø-umba ndøgo, Boaz gesuzam. Kha wageap-ús. Yaq ndego gendoqambun. Yaq nqáe! Sævam kopo, teqa zenda tokhotapak-te gusømbimám.
וַיְהִי֙ בַּחֲצִ֣י הַלַּ֔יְלָה וַיֶּחֱרַ֥ד הָאִ֖ישׁ וַיִּלָּפֵ֑ת וְהִנֵּ֣ה אִשָּׁ֔ה שֹׁכֶ֖בֶת מַרְגְּלֹתָֽיו׃
BSB 3:8 At midnight, Boaz was startled, turned over, and there lying at his feet was a woman!

Ruth 3: translator's notes

What this draft is

A machine draft of Ruth 3 in Kuni (kvg), made 14.10.2026 from the Hebrew (orig/heb/rut003.txt, 18 verses, Hebrew numbering = BSB numbering) with the BSB as the only assisting translation. The drafter knows no Kuni: every word, suffix and construction was taken from the Kuni NT, first through the verse pack (_learn/packs/rut003.md, built from the store profile/decisions.json / profile/observations.json and the lexicon), then through _learn/concord.py for what the pack lacked, and from chapters 1 and 2 (rut001.txt, rut002.txt), whose renderings are reused verbatim wherever the Hebrew repeats (Ngólam, tugu zakhapuka, nigi ekeza ezoqám, sasae mbasønakheis, ezoqa ndakinak, Evezøza qo bèkhakheinøqavém, khakha-mbá, nqovotaz-qa, Betliem vemiav-té genøwáv, qanaqanus, nqægo khaiya, Moab manqei-ak sævam is not needed here). Confidence per verse: high = every piece is an attested NT form or a direct substitution in an attested frame; medium = one or two forms built by analogy (prefix swap for person/gender, an imperative or optative built on an attested stem); low = a construction without a close model. Status: experiment, not reviewed; no footnotes.

Exegetical observations

  • v1 מָנוֹחַ "resting place" (a home with a husband) is "pusup manqei", the NT's "place of rest" (Heb 4:1-11); chapter 1 v9 had "pusup" + "zivis-qá khoev-te" for מְנוּחָה. The rhetorical "should I not seek" is a statement of intent; the NT turns such questions into statements (Luk 13:16) or asks them with the negated verb ("Gê, no Yesu ometeâv?", 1Co 9:1), and I found no deontic 1sg form to negate.
  • v2 הֲלֹא "is not Boaz ...?" = "Nqáe!" + statement, the NT's attention marker (ch2 v8 "Listen"). Threshing floor: the NT's "wit evem nonqo" (wheat-winnowing place, Luk 3:17) with bali; "winnow" = evem (Luk 3:17 "ndǿ-evém"); the Hebrew participle "is winnowing tonight" is a future ("will winnow tonight").
  • v3 The three preparations are three chained imperatives (Act 22:16 pattern). "Perfume" is the NT's "tae et sisip mbomambaqape" (Jhn 12:3 nard); "anoint/put on" = qouz, which also means "pour" (Jhn 13:5) and is reused in v15. "Do not let the man know you are there" has no jussive-passive model; "ta ezoqam ndøgo, qo mè꞉-otevák" says "the man must not notice you" with the negative future of otev (1Co 2:11); the same form carries v14 "do not let it be known".
  • v4, 7, 8, 13, 14 "lie down" (שׁכב) is the sleep stem sømbi, the NT's word for lying on a mat (Act 9:33) and sleeping (Mat 25:5); the gender/number forms gusømbimám, genø-sømbiám and the imperatives are built. "Uncover his feet" is made concrete as "take the cloth off his feet" (khofoe, the verb of Act 7:33 "take off your sandals" and Act 22:23 "threw off their cloaks").
  • v7 "the heap of grain" (עֲרֵמָה) has no NT word: "bali nqeitam" (stored/piled barley) from the store-stem nqeit (barn, Luk 3:17). "Was in good spirits" = "khanakhanakh ndøndáp" (became glad).
  • v8 "startled" (חרד, trembled) = "woke up" (gesuzam, Act 16:27) + "Kha wageap-ús." (trembling, Luk 8:47); "turned over" (נלפת) = "gendoqambun", the NT's "turned (to look)".
  • v9 "spread your wing/garment over me": the BSB's main text (garment) is followed with the NT's "cover" (gutav) in a polite request (Ambá ... -ɨn); a reviewer may prefer the wing image ("qoqa taf mokho-te"), which would echo Boaz's blessing in 2:12.
  • v9, 12, 13 גֹּאֵל keeps chapter 2's description (a relative who is there to redeem us, nqovotaz-qa) in predicate form: "qo nigi ekeza ezoqám, ni nqovotaz-qa nqoqote꞉go" (you are our relative, to redeem us); "redeem you" uses the 2sg-object optative/future built on nqovotaz (betaqá-, søtaqá-).
  • v10 "more kindness now than before" uses the comparison frame of 1:13 (A, B mø̀ndøløvú); "run after the young men" is "went after" (okhoeav, did not go); "whether rich or poor" uses the NT's "oskiá ndøgo, A o B" (Mrk 12:14) with the NT's own words for rich and poor.
  • v11 אֵשֶׁת חַיִל "woman of noble character": "sævam eqeieqeí" (a worthy woman, after "ezoqa eqeieqeí" 1Co 15:9) plus "Qoqa matev eqeieqeí" (your character is good, 1Co 15:33); "all the gate of my people" = "all the people of my village" (Mrk 1:33). "They know that" is the 2Co 4:2 frame.
  • v12 "a redeemer nearer than I": "near" (avønín) is spatial/temporal in the NT; closeness of kin is expressed as "he is near to you; he exceeds me in that" (the Act 13:25 "he surpasses me" idea with 1Jn 3:20's mø̀ndøløvú).
  • v13 "as surely as the LORD lives": no NT counterpart; the swearing frame "I swear to God thus:" (Act 23:12-14, Mat 10:32) carries the oath but not "lives". "Stay/lodge here tonight" and "wait" (v18 שְׁבִי) are both "qanøqóm" (sit, stay).
  • v14 "before anyone could recognize another" = "early, while it was still dark, she got up; no one recognized that it was Ruth", with the NT's "did not recognize that it was Jesus" (Jhn 21:4).
  • v15 The shawl (מִטְפַּחַת) is the NT's "ndabua qozaq nonqo" (wrap-cloth, cloak); "hold it out" = "stretch it out" (Luk 6:10's hand); "six measures" = "bali yakhav siks" with the basket/ measure unit of 2:17 (the real amount, about 40 l, is not conveyed); "placed it on her" = "put it on her shoulders" (Luk 15:5). "He went into the city": the BSB's masculine reading (the Hebrew ketiv) is kept.
  • v16 מִי־אַתְּ "who are you?" is read with the BSB as "how did it go?" ("Gê, áv khané꞉go, qo-te?").
  • v18 "he will not rest unless he has resolved the matter today" = "he will not rest until he has finished this matter today" (pusa gétayák, built from "yaq-fia gétayák" will not hold judgment, Mat 23:16); "how things go" = "what happens" (tøfakhan).

Open questions

  1. v1 "should I not seek a resting place for you?": rendered as a statement of intent ("No qo-te pusup manqei vawa-qa nombogó", built after "yoge-qa nombogó", Act 25:22). Is there a natural rhetorical form for "ought I not …?", and is "pusup manqei" usable for a home?
  2. Threshing floor "bali evem nonqo", winnow "evem", heap "bali nqeitam": the first two follow Luk 3:17's "wit evem nonqo", the heap is built; a speaker should supply the real farming words (see also chapter 2's gleaning and sheaves).
  3. "Lie down" as the sleep verb sømbi, with the built forms gusømbimám, genø-sømbiám and the imperatives vø̄sømbiam / vø̀sømbiám; "uncover his feet" as "take the cloth off his feet".
  4. v3, 14 "do not let the man know / do not let it be known": "X mè꞉-otevák" (X will/must not notice) rests on 1Co 2:11; the mood (jussive) and the grave on mè꞉ need checking.
  5. v9 "spread your garment over me" as the request "Ambá qoqa ndabua-te qonø-gutavɨ́n"; the wing reading (taf) is the alternative. The kinsman-redeemer still has no term; v9, 12, 13 use the chapter 2 description in predicate form and the built 2sg-object forms betaqá-nqovotáz / søtaqá-nqovotáz.
  6. v12 "a redeemer nearer than I" paraphrased ("he is near to you; he exceeds me in that"): is there a kinship word for "closer relative"?
  7. v13 "as surely as the LORD lives": the NT swearing frame "no Evezøza-te, āv qaté-unimanqatín nqǽgo:" drops "lives"; a Kuni oath idiom may exist.
  8. v15 "six measures" = "bali yakhav siks": the unit is the basket of 2:17; the amount is unknown to the reader. "Placed it on her" = "put it on her shoulders" (nqanqa, Luk 15:5).
  9. v16 "How did it go?" as "Gê, áv khané꞉go, qo-te?" (how is it with you): built from Act 2:8; tone and idiom unverified. The Hebrew "who are you?" could also be kept as in v9.
  10. v18 "he will not rest" = "pusa gétayák", from "yaq-fia gétayák" (will not hold judgment); the stem ta is the same, the collocation is not attested. "Unless he has resolved the matter today" is rendered "until he has finished this matter today".
  11. "Until morning" = "atema qanaqanús" (after "atema khagús"); the NT's own "until daybreak" is "atema꞉ vøndōpave" (Act 20:11), whose spelling (꞉ after atema) I did not copy.
