Ruth 3: translator's notes

What this draft is

A machine draft of Ruth 3 in Kuni (kvg), made 14.10.2026 from the Hebrew (orig/heb/rut003.txt, 18 verses, Hebrew numbering = BSB numbering) with the BSB as the only assisting translation. The drafter knows no Kuni: every word, suffix and construction was taken from the Kuni NT, first through the verse pack (_learn/packs/rut003.md, built from the store profile/decisions.json / profile/observations.json and the lexicon), then through _learn/concord.py for what the pack lacked, and from chapters 1 and 2 (rut001.txt, rut002.txt), whose renderings are reused verbatim wherever the Hebrew repeats (Ngólam, tugu zakhapuka, nigi ekeza ezoqám, sasae mbasønakheis, ezoqa ndakinak, Evezøza qo bèkhakheinøqavém, khakha-mbá, nqovotaz-qa, Betliem vemiav-té genøwáv, qanaqanus, nqægo khaiya, Moab manqei-ak sævam is not needed here). Confidence per verse: high = every piece is an attested NT form or a direct substitution in an attested frame; medium = one or two forms built by analogy (prefix swap for person/gender, an imperative or optative built on an attested stem); low = a construction without a close model. Status: experiment, not reviewed; no footnotes.

Exegetical observations

  • v1 מָנוֹחַ "resting place" (a home with a husband) is "pusup manqei", the NT's "place of rest" (Heb 4:1-11); chapter 1 v9 had "pusup" + "zivis-qá khoev-te" for מְנוּחָה. The rhetorical "should I not seek" is a statement of intent; the NT turns such questions into statements (Luk 13:16) or asks them with the negated verb ("Gê, no Yesu ometeâv?", 1Co 9:1), and I found no deontic 1sg form to negate.
  • v2 הֲלֹא "is not Boaz ...?" = "Nqáe!" + statement, the NT's attention marker (ch2 v8 "Listen"). Threshing floor: the NT's "wit evem nonqo" (wheat-winnowing place, Luk 3:17) with bali; "winnow" = evem (Luk 3:17 "ndǿ-evém"); the Hebrew participle "is winnowing tonight" is a future ("will winnow tonight").
  • v3 The three preparations are three chained imperatives (Act 22:16 pattern). "Perfume" is the NT's "tae et sisip mbomambaqape" (Jhn 12:3 nard); "anoint/put on" = qouz, which also means "pour" (Jhn 13:5) and is reused in v15. "Do not let the man know you are there" has no jussive-passive model; "ta ezoqam ndøgo, qo mè꞉-otevák" says "the man must not notice you" with the negative future of otev (1Co 2:11); the same form carries v14 "do not let it be known".
  • v4, 7, 8, 13, 14 "lie down" (שׁכב) is the sleep stem sømbi, the NT's word for lying on a mat (Act 9:33) and sleeping (Mat 25:5); the gender/number forms gusømbimám, genø-sømbiám and the imperatives are built. "Uncover his feet" is made concrete as "take the cloth off his feet" (khofoe, the verb of Act 7:33 "take off your sandals" and Act 22:23 "threw off their cloaks").
  • v7 "the heap of grain" (עֲרֵמָה) has no NT word: "bali nqeitam" (stored/piled barley) from the store-stem nqeit (barn, Luk 3:17). "Was in good spirits" = "khanakhanakh ndøndáp" (became glad).
  • v8 "startled" (חרד, trembled) = "woke up" (gesuzam, Act 16:27) + "Kha wageap-ús." (trembling, Luk 8:47); "turned over" (נלפת) = "gendoqambun", the NT's "turned (to look)".
  • v9 "spread your wing/garment over me": the BSB's main text (garment) is followed with the NT's "cover" (gutav) in a polite request (Ambá ... -ɨn); a reviewer may prefer the wing image ("qoqa taf mokho-te"), which would echo Boaz's blessing in 2:12.
  • v9, 12, 13 גֹּאֵל keeps chapter 2's description (a relative who is there to redeem us, nqovotaz-qa) in predicate form: "qo nigi ekeza ezoqám, ni nqovotaz-qa nqoqote꞉go" (you are our relative, to redeem us); "redeem you" uses the 2sg-object optative/future built on nqovotaz (betaqá-, søtaqá-).
  • v10 "more kindness now than before" uses the comparison frame of 1:13 (A, B mø̀ndøløvú); "run after the young men" is "went after" (okhoeav, did not go); "whether rich or poor" uses the NT's "oskiá ndøgo, A o B" (Mrk 12:14) with the NT's own words for rich and poor.
  • v11 אֵשֶׁת חַיִל "woman of noble character": "sævam eqeieqeí" (a worthy woman, after "ezoqa eqeieqeí" 1Co 15:9) plus "Qoqa matev eqeieqeí" (your character is good, 1Co 15:33); "all the gate of my people" = "all the people of my village" (Mrk 1:33). "They know that" is the 2Co 4:2 frame.
  • v12 "a redeemer nearer than I": "near" (avønín) is spatial/temporal in the NT; closeness of kin is expressed as "he is near to you; he exceeds me in that" (the Act 13:25 "he surpasses me" idea with 1Jn 3:20's mø̀ndøløvú).
  • v13 "as surely as the LORD lives": no NT counterpart; the swearing frame "I swear to God thus:" (Act 23:12-14, Mat 10:32) carries the oath but not "lives". "Stay/lodge here tonight" and "wait" (v18 שְׁבִי) are both "qanøqóm" (sit, stay).
  • v14 "before anyone could recognize another" = "early, while it was still dark, she got up; no one recognized that it was Ruth", with the NT's "did not recognize that it was Jesus" (Jhn 21:4).
  • v15 The shawl (מִטְפַּחַת) is the NT's "ndabua qozaq nonqo" (wrap-cloth, cloak); "hold it out" = "stretch it out" (Luk 6:10's hand); "six measures" = "bali yakhav siks" with the basket/ measure unit of 2:17 (the real amount, about 40 l, is not conveyed); "placed it on her" = "put it on her shoulders" (Luk 15:5). "He went into the city": the BSB's masculine reading (the Hebrew ketiv) is kept.
  • v16 מִי־אַתְּ "who are you?" is read with the BSB as "how did it go?" ("Gê, áv khané꞉go, qo-te?").
  • v18 "he will not rest unless he has resolved the matter today" = "he will not rest until he has finished this matter today" (pusa gétayák, built from "yaq-fia gétayák" will not hold judgment, Mat 23:16); "how things go" = "what happens" (tøfakhan).

  • Divine name as chapters 1-2: Evezøza for YHWH (vv. 10, 13); no Elohim in this chapter.
  • Speech frames as before: "āv gumbøe-eín nqǽgo" (v1), "gumbo-ein" (vv. 10, 15, 18), "gundu-qavøiu" (vv. 5, 9), "gumbobevap" (asked her, vv. 9, 16; Act 5:8), "āv gene-eín nqǽgo" (v14), "āv guni-eín nqǽgo" (v17), "āv genømbe-eín nqǽgo" (he said to me, v17); orders as "manqa ... -løvua" (heading, v6; 19 verses with manqa + løvua).

New name forms

None. Lut, Boaz, Neomi, Betliem as in chapters 1-2; "Lut-gu zakhapuka" (Ruth's mother-in-law) follows "Saemon-gu zimpuka" (Luk 4:38).

Open questions

  1. v1 "should I not seek a resting place for you?": rendered as a statement of intent ("No qo-te pusup manqei vawa-qa nombogó", built after "yoge-qa nombogó", Act 25:22). Is there a natural rhetorical form for "ought I not …?", and is "pusup manqei" usable for a home?
  2. Threshing floor "bali evem nonqo", winnow "evem", heap "bali nqeitam": the first two follow Luk 3:17's "wit evem nonqo", the heap is built; a speaker should supply the real farming words (see also chapter 2's gleaning and sheaves).
  3. "Lie down" as the sleep verb sømbi, with the built forms gusømbimám, genø-sømbiám and the imperatives vø̄sømbiam / vø̀sømbiám; "uncover his feet" as "take the cloth off his feet".
  4. v3, 14 "do not let the man know / do not let it be known": "X mè꞉-otevák" (X will/must not notice) rests on 1Co 2:11; the mood (jussive) and the grave on mè꞉ need checking.
  5. v9 "spread your garment over me" as the request "Ambá qoqa ndabua-te qonø-gutavɨ́n"; the wing reading (taf) is the alternative. The kinsman-redeemer still has no term; v9, 12, 13 use the chapter 2 description in predicate form and the built 2sg-object forms betaqá-nqovotáz / søtaqá-nqovotáz.
  6. v12 "a redeemer nearer than I" paraphrased ("he is near to you; he exceeds me in that"): is there a kinship word for "closer relative"?
  7. v13 "as surely as the LORD lives": the NT swearing frame "no Evezøza-te, āv qaté-unimanqatín nqǽgo:" drops "lives"; a Kuni oath idiom may exist.
  8. v15 "six measures" = "bali yakhav siks": the unit is the basket of 2:17; the amount is unknown to the reader. "Placed it on her" = "put it on her shoulders" (nqanqa, Luk 15:5).
  9. v16 "How did it go?" as "Gê, áv khané꞉go, qo-te?" (how is it with you): built from Act 2:8; tone and idiom unverified. The Hebrew "who are you?" could also be kept as in v9.
  10. v18 "he will not rest" = "pusa gétayák", from "yaq-fia gétayák" (will not hold judgment); the stem ta is the same, the collocation is not attested. "Unless he has resolved the matter today" is rendered "until he has finished this matter today".
  11. "Until morning" = "atema qanaqanús" (after "atema khagús"); the NT's own "until daybreak" is "atema꞉ vøndōpave" (Act 20:11), whose spelling (꞉ after atema) I did not copy.
