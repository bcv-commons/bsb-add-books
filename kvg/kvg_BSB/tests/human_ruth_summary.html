What the human Kuni Ruth (and Jonah) teach us: summary, 2026-10-14

CC0; quotations from the Kuni texts credited in ../CREDITS.md. The details are in local/human_ruth_analysis.md (local only: it prints much of the human text; generated by a local analysis script) and alignment_report.md. The human texts are eBible kvg (2015). Our drafts are the four test chapters in ruth-test/, written by agents that knew only the Kuni NT.

1. The ceiling of learning from the NT alone

tokens also in the NTword types also in the NTword pairs also in the NT
human Ruth89%75%51%
human Jonah93%83%62%
our Ruth drafts96%94%63%
  • About 11% of the human Ruth's words (25% of its word forms) don't occur in the NT. Most of them are names (Naomi, Elimelek, Malon, Kilion, Opa), Yawe, and the inflected verb forms a new story needs. An agent can only build these by rule.
  • Our drafts stay inside the NT (96%), as the rules require. 27 of our word forms occur nowhere in the corpus: built forms such as nqovotaz forms and the sømbi "lie down" forms, and our Neomi and Eflata.
  • Only half of the human Ruth's word pairs occur in the NT. That is the main reason any NT-only draft stays far from the human text: the words can be known, their combinations mostly cannot.

  • Verse length, sentence length and the number of Hebrew clauses per Kuni sentence are close to the human translators'. The clause-line approach gives the right rhythm.
  • The connectors are off in three ways:
    • our agents use zapa ndǿgo three times as often as the translators: they render every Hebrew כִּי;
    • they use geté half as often;
    • they use neka like the NT, not like the OT books.
  • Our drafts carry fewer tone marks: the agents dropped marks where they built forms. Tone is the part of the language our agents can learn least.

3. The store against the human text

  • Of 194 live terms, formulas and names, 101 are confirmed by the human Ruth or Jonah, 60 differ, and 33 have no evidence either way. These are mechanical counts, and the curation agent is checking them one by one.
  • Divine names: the human Ruth writes YHWH as \*Yawe (18 times, a glossary word), the human Jonah as Evezøza, as our agents did. The OT books don't agree, so the Kuni team has to decide.
  • Names:
    • the human Ruth has Naomi where we had Neomi, and Eflat where we had Eflata;
    • our Lut, Opa, Malon, Kilion, Elimelek and the built Lia (Leah) are all right;
    • Rachel is Laisol, as in Mat 2:18.

4. How the translators solved our gaps

The human translators did the opposite of what our rules ask: where Kuni culture has no concept, they explain rather than imitate.

  • Measures in modern units: the ephah is "about 10 kilograms" (Ande āv 10 kilogrém, Ruth 2:17), the six measures of barley "about 20 kilograms" (3:15). Our agents used the NT's basket unit.
  • Explanations inside the text:
    • the redeemer is introduced with the law of Moses (Mozes-qa guguna manqat āv qane-eín …, 2:20) instead of a single term;
    • the barley harvest comes with a bracketed note (1:22).
  • Concrete descriptions:
    • threshing floor: bali nanga tøkuza manqei;
    • gleaning: bali khakhayakh … pisam;
    • sister-in-law: qakhapikia-pakha-gu zas, "your brother-in-law's wife".
  • Free renderings of idioms:
    • "Boaz was startled" becomes "Boaz woke up" (tènesuzám), close to our agent's choice;
    • חֶסֶד is matev mbomømbomá, not our tanakh matev.

5. What this means for drafting other OT books

  1. Lift the "attested in the NT" rule to "attested in the corpus" (done), and let explanatory additions be allowed where the Kuni team does so. Ask the team whether that is their practice for all books or a feature of the 2015 Ruth.
  2. Teach the connectors from the OT books: geté more, zapa ndǿgo only where the reason matters. The wiki's new OT-narrative page carries the figures.
  3. Copy the tone marks from the model verses. Built forms need a marked pattern from the store.
  4. Use the human names and the human solutions as decided entries (the curation agent supersedes ours).
  5. The Ruth chapter averages of our drafts (chrF per verse against the human text: 47.0, 46.7, 45.9, 52.8) are the baseline. The next test is a book without a human translation, so quality can only be judged by the Kuni team.
